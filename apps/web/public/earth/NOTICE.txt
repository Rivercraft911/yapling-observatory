The publisher credits NASA Blue Marble and other geodata. These are static, adjusted composites, not live weather; no single observation epoch is supplied. No NASA endorsement is implied.

Global day and cloud maps are the unmodified original 8192x4096JPEGs from the publisher. GPUs supporting less than 8192px use the preserved 4096x2048fallbacks. Fallback changes: Lanczos downsampling, grayscale PNG cloud conversion. Ocean/specular mask converted from TIFF to grayscale PNG. Rendering adjusts saturation, lighting, cloud opacity and material response. Geography was not generated or redrawn. See asset-provenance.json for exact sources, sizes and SHA256 hashes.

Rendering: a display-only approximate atmosphere and Sun direction, independent of RF, weather, orbital dynamics and delivered data. The orbit arc is only the returned synthetic Run history. Locator sizes are exaggerated.

Preserved regional cloud derivative (superseded runtime)

The 2048 × 2048 grayscale PNG is a native crop of NASA's original 21600 × 21600 western-half cloud image, with no resizing or synthetic detail. It has the same bounds as the regional daytime patch:29.1–46.166666667 degrees N,130.666666667–113.6 degrees W. The published product class is 1 km;angular grid spacing is 1/120 degree. The 2002 NASA description identifies a cloud composite using two days of visible imagery and a third day of thermal-IR imagery over the poles. This retained 2001 composite is not the simulated epoch or live weather, cloud water content, RF loss or thermal-environment truth. That cloud derivative does not include city lights.

The earlier Stanford-only mask is retained with /earth/regional-cloud.json for provenance and reproducibility. It is superseded at runtime by the native tile layer below. No NASA endorsement is implied.


Native cloud tiles for close cameras

NASA Goddard Space Flight Center / Reto Stöckli (clouds); Robert Simmon (compositing enhancements)
https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/the-blue-marble-true-color-global-imagery-at-1km-resolution/
https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/

The original western and eastern 21600 × 21600 grayscale PNGs form a 43200 × 21600 global grid, with native angular spacing 1/120 degree and a published nominal1km product class. The242 runtime WebP derivatives preserve that native sampling; each interior is at most2032 × 2032 pixels, with eight real neighboring-pixel gutters. Longitude gutters wrap and pole gutters clamp to original edge pixels. WebP quality90/method4 introduces documented lossy compression. There is no resizing, sharpening, inpainting or procedural cloud detail. The source2001 multiday composite, known mosaic boundaries and data limitations are retained. These clouds do not describe the simulated date or measured RF/thermal conditions, and are coarser than the500m regional daytime map.

The curated tile set totals86,643,482bytes. The renderer selects up to nine local tiles from the actual close-camera position, with at most two loads in progress and per-tile size/SHA/dimension checks. Single-channel R8 textures limit cloud tile GPU storage to48MiB including mipmaps, plus at most36MiB retained CPU mask data. Two temporary bitmap/canvas decodes add transient memory. Tiles are released on eviction, after15s outside the needed view, and immediately on view disposal. The global8K map remains outside loaded coverage, near some horizon/polar edges, on failure and in far views. Adjacent tiles use native gutters; exposed coverage edges and camera distance feather to global artwork. Visible cloud opacity and approximate surface shadows share the same sampler, coordinates and tile weights. No runtime external requests occur.

See /earth/cloud-tiles.json for every tile identity, native pixel bounds, complete source hashes, acquisition limits, measured compression errors and licensing. This display-only layer is independent of numerical weather, RF, power and thermal models. No NASA endorsement is implied.

Night lights

Solar System Scope / INOVE
https://www.solarsystemscope.com/textures/
https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/

The night-light maps are the unmodified 8192x4096 and 2048x1024 publisher JPEGs, distributed under CC BY 4.0. The 2K original is used when the GPU does not support the 8K texture. Runtime appearance uses a restrained emissive gain and a smooth daylight fade; cloud opacity can obscure the lights. The publisher supplies adjusted composite imagery without a single observation epoch. These maps are not current city activity, simulated-date observations, calibrated light radiance, weather or spacecraft thermal inputs. No buildings, city geometry or procedural light locations are generated. Source URLs, sizes and SHA-256 identities are recorded in asset-provenance.json.

NASA Black Marble 2016 global night lights
Source: https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/earth-at-night/maps/
Credit: NASA Earth Observatory, Black Marble 2016 / Suomi NPP VIIRS.
Use: https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/

The original 13500 x 6750 grayscale JPEG is bundled unchanged. Its native grid covers the full globe at 0.026666667 degrees per pixel; NASA describes the product as 3 km. Runtime R8 storage keeps the complete mip chain to about 115.87 MiB on compatible GPUs, less than the previous 8K RGBA map. A power-0.65 display curve brings out faint existing lights while preserving black and peak values; a warm tint and the existing 0.35 emission gain remain visual choices. This annual composite is not the simulated date, calibrated radiance, weather, RF attenuation or a thermal input. Smaller GPUs and failed loads retain the earlier 8K/2K maps and their credits above. See night-map.json for source hashes and runtime memory bounds.
