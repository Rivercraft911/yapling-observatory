The publisher credits NASA Blue Marble and other geodata. These are static, adjusted composites, not live weather; no single observation epoch is supplied. No NASA endorsement is implied.

Global day and cloud maps are the unmodified original 8192x4096JPEGs from the publisher. GPUs supporting less than 8192px use the preserved 4096x2048fallbacks. Fallback changes: Lanczos downsampling, grayscale PNG cloud conversion. Ocean/specular mask converted from TIFF to grayscale PNG. Rendering adjusts saturation, lighting, cloud opacity and material response. Geography was not generated or redrawn. See asset-provenance.json for exact sources, sizes and SHA256 hashes.

Rendering: a display-only approximate atmosphere and Sun direction, independent of RF, weather, orbital dynamics and delivered data. The orbit arc is only the returned synthetic Run history. Locator sizes are exaggerated.

Regional daytime LOD

NASA Earth Observatory / Reto Stöckli and Robert Simmon
https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/blue-marble-next-generation/base-map/

Blue Marble: Next Generation, August 2004 base map, A1 native 21600 × 21600 source. The runtime 4096 × 4096 JPEG is an exact pixel crop, with no upsampling or invented detail. Geographic coverage:29.1–46.166666667 degrees N,130.666666667–113.6 degrees W. Published nominal product resolution 500 m; angular grid spacing 1/240 degree. Dates describe a monthly composite, not simulated-time imagery or live weather. This patch has separate NASA imagery-use terms; it is not covered by the Solar System Scope CC BY license. NASA content generally is not subject to US copyright and may be used for educational/informational computer simulations with source acknowledgment. No NASA endorsement is implied.

The western-US patch is lazy-loaded only for a visible close camera near its coverage, with soft geographic/distance blending into the preserved global 8K fallback. Outside coverage, only the global map is available. Global clouds remain the static 8K composite;close western-US views can use the separately identified NASA regional cloud layer below. See regional-lod.json for exact source, crop bounds, bytes, transformations and SHA-256 identities.

Regional cloud coverage LOD

NASA Goddard Space Flight Center / Reto Stöckli (clouds); Robert Simmon (compositing enhancements)
https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/the-blue-marble-true-color-global-imagery-at-1km-resolution/
https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/

The 2048 × 2048 grayscale PNG is a native crop of NASA's original 21600 × 21600 western-half cloud image, with no resizing or synthetic detail. It has the same bounds as the regional daytime patch:29.1–46.166666667 degrees N,130.666666667–113.6 degrees W. The published product class is 1 km;angular grid spacing is 1/120 degree. The 2002 NASA description identifies a cloud composite using two days of visible imagery and a third day of thermal-IR imagery over the poles. This retained 2001 composite is not the simulated epoch or live weather, cloud water content, RF loss or thermal-environment truth. No city-light imagery is used.

The regional mask and its surface shadow share the same source, coordinates and blend weight. Each NASA patch loads/fails/cancels independently;global imagery remains the fallback. Soft geographic and camera blending joins different composites rather than implying a contemporaneous weather mosaic. See /earth/regional-cloud.json for original/crop SHA-256 identities, exact bounds, dates, byte sizes and transformation. Combined extra regional day/cloud texture storage is at most106.67 MiB including mipmaps. No NASA endorsement is implied.
